How this works: Each block below is timed. Close all notes before starting. Attempt every question, then check your answers using the mark notes provided. This is not about getting everything right — it's about finding out what sticks and what doesn't under exam conditions.

Covered in the planner this week = full questions. On the school list but not revised this week = quick scan questions at the end — just enough to flag gaps.

⏱ Maths — 30 minutes · no notes · no calculator for first 10 min
Q1. Solve simultaneously: 3x + 2y = 16 and x − y = 2 Simultaneous equations
✓ x=4, y=2 — elimination: multiply eq2 by 2, add to eq1
Q2. Rearrange to make r the subject: A = πr² Manipulation of formulae
✓ r = √(A/π) — divide by π, then square root
Q3. Solve: x² + 5x + 6 = 0 Quadratic equations
✓ (x+2)(x+3) = 0 → x = −2 or x = −3
Q4. A rectangle has area 48 cm². One side is (x+2). The other is (x−2). Find x. Expanding + quadratics
✓ (x+2)(x−2) = x²−4 = 48 → x² = 52 → x = √52 ≈ 7.2
Q5. A trapezium has parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, height 4 cm. Find its area. Area / mensuration
✓ A = ½(6+10)×4 = 32 cm²
Q6. A triangle has sides 7 cm and 9 cm with included angle 54°. Find the area. Trigonometry / area
✓ A = ½ × 7 × 9 × sin54° = ½ × 63 × 0.809 ≈ 25.5 cm²
⏱ Physics — 25 minutes · no notes
Q1. A substance has mass 240 g and volume 80 cm³. Calculate its density. State the unit. Introduction: density
✓ ρ = m/V = 240/80 = 3 g/cm³
Q2. Name the three types of nuclear radiation. State one property of each. Nuclear Physics
✓ Alpha (helium nucleus, stopped by paper), Beta (electron, stopped by aluminium), Gamma (EM wave, reduced by lead)
Q3. A radioactive sample has a half-life of 4 hours. It starts with 800 atoms. How many remain after 12 hours? Nuclear Physics: half-life
✓ 3 half-lives: 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 atoms
Q4. State the difference between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. Nuclear Physics
✓ Fission: splitting a large nucleus. Fusion: joining two small nuclei. Both release energy.
Q5. A force of 150 N acts on an area of 0.5 m². Calculate the pressure. Introduction: pressure
✓ P = F/A = 150/0.5 = 300 Pa
⏱ Chemistry — 25 minutes · no notes
Q1. Balance this equation and add state symbols: Mg + O₂ → MgO Equations: balancing + state symbols
✓ 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s) — state symbols often carry 1 dedicated mark
Q2. Approximately what percentage of the atmosphere is: (a) nitrogen (b) oxygen (c) carbon dioxide? Atmosphere
✓ N₂ ≈ 78%, O₂ ≈ 21%, CO₂ ≈ 0.04%
Q3. What is the greenhouse effect? Name two greenhouse gases. Atmosphere
✓ Greenhouse gases trap heat/infrared radiation re-emitted from Earth's surface. CO₂ and methane (CH₄).
Q4. Explain the difference between ionic and covalent bonding. Give one example of each. Bonding
✓ Ionic: electron transfer between metal + non-metal (e.g. NaCl). Covalent: electron sharing between non-metals (e.g. H₂O).
Q5. Write a word equation for the combustion of methane. Equations: types of reaction
✓ Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Exam Technique

The most common ways marks are lost — not because you don't know the content, but because of how you answer. Read this before every exam.

Maths
Show all workingEven if your final answer is wrong, method marks are available. Never skip steps.
Check unitsDensity in g/cm³ or kg/m³. Area in cm² or m². Always write the unit — marks are lost without it.
Re-read the questionHighlight what's being asked. Many marks lost by answering the wrong thing (e.g. finding x when they asked for 2x+1).
Don't leave blanksIf stuck, write the formula. Substituting numbers into the right formula often earns a mark even without the final answer.
Check your answer makes senseA negative length or a probability over 1 means something went wrong.
Physics
Write the equation firstState the formula before substituting. Examiners award a mark for the correct equation even if the arithmetic is wrong.
Units are mandatoryNo unit = no mark on calculation questions. Memorise: Pa for pressure, kg/m³ for density, m/s for speed, J for energy.
"Describe" vs "Explain"Describe = say what happens. Explain = say why it happens. Missing the "why" loses the second mark.
6-mark questions need structureUse: what happens → why it happens → what the effect is. Cover all three or lose marks.
Standard form in calculationsDon't round too early — keep 3 significant figures throughout, round only at the end.
Chemistry
State symbols matter(s), (l), (g), (aq) — often 1 mark in equation questions. Don't omit them.
Balancing — never change subscriptsOnly change the big number in front. Changing subscripts changes the compound entirely.
"Suggest" questions are opinion-free"Suggest" means use your scientific knowledge to give a reasoned answer — not a guess. Link to a principle you know.
Rate of reaction questionsAlways link to collision theory: more collisions = faster rate. State frequency and energy of collisions.
Read graphs carefullyRate = gradient of a concentration-time graph. Steeper = faster. Flat = reaction stopped.
Command words — what the examiner actually wants
State / Name / GiveOne word or one sentence. No explanation needed.
DescribeSay what you observe or what happens. No need to explain why.
ExplainGive a reason. Use "because" or "therefore". This is always worth more marks.
CalculateShow formula → substitute → calculate → unit. All four steps.
SuggestApply knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. Use a scientific principle.
CompareState both similarities AND differences. Don't just describe one.
EvaluateGive evidence for and against, then reach a conclusion.
SketchA rough graph is fine — but label axes and mark key points.
